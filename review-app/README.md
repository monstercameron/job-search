# Job review

Double-click **Start Job Review.cmd** and open http://127.0.0.1:8080/ if the browser does not open. Keep its command window open. Press Ctrl+C in that window to stop the app.

The top bar has six sections on every screen: **Jobs**, **Next up**, **Applications**, **Profile**, **Résumé** and **Metrics**. Moving between screens cross-fades in browsers that support view transitions (current Chrome and Edge): the tab mark slides to the new tab, and a job's title and score travel from the list to its application page. Turning on the system's reduce-motion setting switches all motion off.

Colours mean the same thing on every screen, and each coloured item also says its meaning in words. Green is moving forward: stages from Applied through Accepted, a strong match (80 or more) and low priority. Yellow is waiting or worth a check: Research, Interested and Preparing, an upcoming follow-up, a posting not confirmed open, a fair match (65 to 79) and medium priority. Red is a problem or urgent: Rejected and Withdrawn, a follow-up that is due, high priority, a weak match (under 65), required skills missing from the résumé, and errors. Blue marks the app's own controls and headings.

**Jobs** lists your leads best match first. Each row shows the provisional score out of 100 with a five-part bar (role direction 30, qualifications 25, location 20, pay 15, still open 10): each segment's width is that part's share of the 100 and its fill is what the job earned. Open **Score breakdown and details** for the exact points, what to check before applying, the role, and company research links. A yellow "Posting not confirmed open" flag means the saved posting status asks for a recheck. Search and filter, pick a sort, and press **Show jobs**; the address bar then holds that exact view, so the browser's back button and the application page's back link return to it. The **Workplace** (Remote, Hybrid, In-office) and **Job type** (Full-time, Contract) toggles stay visible on every screen size; turn on any number and press **Show jobs**. Within a group a job needs one of the toggles that are on, and across groups it needs both, so Remote plus Contract lists remote contract roles. The categories are read from the saved work arrangement, falling back to the location when the arrangement names none, and from the employment type ("Regular" counts as full-time; contractor and B2B count as contract). A role offered as "Remote / Hybrid" counts as both. Text that says the arrangement is unknown, such as "not specified" or "verify", is not guessed at: those jobs appear under **Not stated**. The row badges use the same categories.

**Next up** shows one job at a time from the jobs you have not applied to, best match first, with ties broken by priority. A job leaves the list once its stage is Applied or later (Screening, Interviewing, Offer, Accepted), once it is Rejected, Withdrawn or Closed, or once it has an applied date. It also leaves when you press **Not interested**: the next job moves into its place and a notice offers **Undo**. Not interested only hides the job from Next up; its status and any application stay as they were. The job still appears on Jobs with a "Not interested" flag, and its application page has **Put back in Next up**. Dismissals are kept in the `job_dismissals` table. Each job shows its score and breakdown, flags, location, pay and type, what to check before applying, why it may fit, the role, notes and research links, all open. **Previous** and **Next** move through the list; the address (`/next?n=3`) holds the position, so Back and reload return to it, and **Review and track** opens the job's application page with a link back to the same place. Because the position is a place in the list, applying to a job moves the jobs after it up by one.

**Metrics** (`/metrics`) draws how the search is going from the tracker's own tables. Every chart is inline SVG written in Go (no script, no chart library, nothing loaded from the network) with a title, labelled values, an aria-label and a **Show as a table** disclosure holding the same numbers. It shows a six-number summary; a **pipeline funnel** (tracked, applied, heard back, screening or later, interview, offer, each the share of the row above); the **response rate** with its 95% range; **applications per week** for the last 12 weeks; the **match-score distribution** in 5-point bands (red under 65, yellow 65 to 79, green 80 and up, matching the score colours); **leads by company market**; **leads by work arrangement** (same rules as the Jobs filters; Remote / Hybrid counts under both); and **listing age when added** (date added minus posting date, only for leads that have a posting date, with the coverage stated). An application counts as applied when it has an applied date or a stage from Applied on. It counts as a reply when its stage reaches Screening or later, it is Rejected, or an interview is logged; an automatic confirmation email is not a reply. Rates always show their sample size, and the range shown with a rate is a 95% Wilson interval, so one application reads as "0 to 79%".

**Résumé A/B** (on the Metrics page) compares outcomes per résumé version: applied, got a response, screening or later, interview, offer and rejected, as counts and as rates, side by side, each with its n. A version is a short label on the application (`applications.resume_variant`, a nullable column added at startup). Labels are grouped ignoring capitals. When an application has no label, the job's `resume_version` field is used if it has one; otherwise it is counted as "Not labelled" and never compared. To start a test: pick two or more names, then give every application one, either by asking the assistant to pass `variant` to `save_tailored_resume` or `resume_variant` to `track_application` (or `update_application`), or by typing it in **Label your applications** on the Metrics page. Until each version has 10 applied applications the page shows counts only and says it is too early to conclude anything. Once two or more versions have 10 each it compares response rates with a 95% interval for the difference (Newcombe's method) and says whether the interval excludes zero. It makes that one comparison only, and warns that the jobs themselves differ.

Sorting by priority uses rank (High, Medium, Low), status uses pipeline order (Research through Closed), and salary uses the annualized minimum (hourly × 2,080), so contract rates line up with salaries. Best match, highest pay and the date sorts run highest or newest first. Blank fields stay last.

The app reads and writes the parent folder's `job_search.sqlite3`. At startup it only adds missing tables and columns, never removes any, and makes no backups: copy the database file before upgrading. It refuses a database file that does not exist; on a first run, pass `-init` once to create it with an empty `jobs` table (the other tables are added at every startup).

To test: `go test ./...` (uses a throwaway database, never the real one). To rebuild: install Go, open a terminal in this folder and run `go build -o job-review-mcp.exe .`. Use `go run .` as an alternative. The first build may download Go dependencies. The pages use HTMLeX (https://github.com/monstercameron/HTMLeX, v1.2.3, embedded under `static/htmlex/`; see its `LICENSE`) for partial updates. Searching, filtering and paging replace only the job list. Every save on the application, Résumé and Profile pages replaces only the affected part and moves focus to the confirmation. Each URL answers a browser navigation with the full page and an HTMLeX request (told apart by the `Sec-Fetch-Mode` header) with `<fragment target="…">` blocks plus a `Push-Url` or `Replace-Url` header, so the address bar, reload and Back always match what is shown. With JavaScript off, the same forms and links work by full page loads. The embedded runtime carries local patches, each marked "Local patch (job review app)":
- Swaps run even in embedded browsers that never fire `requestAnimationFrame`.
- `preventDefault` is called correctly, so a form no longer also submits natively.
- `Push-Url`/`Replace-Url` response headers update the address bar.
- Back and Forward reload the page when the path or query changes.
- Plain left clicks on links with `get=` swap instead of navigating.
- Swapped-in `autofocus` elements receive focus.
- Busy elements carry `aria-busy`.

The app listens on localhost by default. It does not submit applications or send messages. To use a different database or port, run `job-review-mcp.exe -db "..\job_search.sqlite3" -addr 127.0.0.1:8081`.

## Application tracking

Choose **Review and track** on a job (or click its title) to open its application page; the job's facts and score stay in the right-hand column while you work. **Where it stands** holds the stage, dates, contact and notes; **Start tracking** (later **Save status**) adds it to Applications. **Activity** is one timeline of messages, interviews and status changes: log new ones with **Log a message** or **Add an interview**, and edit any logged entry in place. Every save returns you to the same section with a confirmation. Messages are notes only: the app does not connect to email or send anything. Dates and times are local and are not synced to a calendar.

**Applications** groups tracked jobs as Follow up now (the follow-up date has arrived), In progress, Not applied yet, and Closed.

Paste your actual résumé on the **Résumé** page; each job's **Résumé match** then compares the job's recorded required skills against it. This is a case-insensitive whole-term check (so "Java" does not count as found inside "JavaScript", and "C" does not match "C#"), not a prediction of any ATS decision; synonyms and compound requirements such as "Go or Java" may be missed. Edit and save named tailored resume versions and cover letters under the job. Use only accurate claims. Documents remain in the SQLite database and may be copied into an external application by you.

The app creates additive `applications`, `interviews`, `communications`, `application_documents`, and `tracker_settings` tables at startup. Saving application status also updates the job status and dates displayed on the job review page. The app is for localhost use: it answers only requests addressed to localhost, 127.0.0.1 or ::1 (so another website cannot read your data through DNS rebinding), and accepts writes only from the same local browser origin.

## Profile

**Profile** holds everything your résumé is built from: name and contact details, summary, experience, skill groups, projects, and education and training. Each part has an **Edit** disclosure. Roles, skill groups, projects and courses can be added, reordered with **Move up** and **Move down**, or removed. Untick **Include on the résumé** to keep an entry for reference but leave it out of the résumé text; Education starts that way. While no education entry is on the résumé, `save_tailored_resume` refuses an education section or a degree, so a tailored résumé cannot claim schooling you left off. The side column holds your search preferences and a list of claims to keep accurate when tailoring. **Copy profile into base résumé** writes the profile as plain text into the base résumé, which every job page's résumé match uses; **Preview the résumé text** shows the text first. Printing the Profile page gives a clean résumé layout without the editing controls.

You can fill it by hand or have an assistant fill it from your published résumé. It is stored in the `profile_fields` and `profile_entries` tables.

## Timeline and confidence

Each application includes a chronological timeline from its applied date, communications, interviews, and status and confidence changes. It shows calendar days between dated events; scheduled interviews can be future events, and undated records appear last. Add your personal confidence and optional explanation in the application form; this is independent of the job-match score. Confidence and status changes are logged as they are saved; changes made before the timeline existed are not reconstructed. Times entered in the tracker are local and not calendar-synced.

Jobs shows 25 per page. Previous page, Next page and the page numbers keep the current filters and sort. Changing filters starts at page 1.

## Company research and markets

Each job's details link to company research. Verified company websites open directly; other website, Wikipedia, Glassdoor, LinkedIn and careers links are labeled searches. Verify the result belongs to the right company. The company market filter and sort use the separate `industry` field, distinct from the job role category. Initial broad classifications are provisional; unclassified companies appear as Unknown. Later startups and imported leads remain Unknown until researched. Existing categories are never overwritten on restart.

## MCP server

The same executable serves the tracker to AI assistants over MCP, so Claude Desktop, Claude Code or ChatGPT can tailor a résumé and apply to a job while this app keeps the record. The assistant does the applying itself: it reads the posting, fills the employer's form in a browser and shows it to you. The tools give it the facts, check and export the résumé, and start the tracking. You press the employer's Submit button yourself, because application forms use CAPTCHAs and the final send is yours.

**Applying to a job** takes three tools, in order:
- `get_application_kit`: the posting, your contact details (including the Profile's mailing address, which is for forms only and never goes into the résumé text) and standard answers (work authorization, availability, relocation), the full Profile including entries kept off the résumé, your "Claims to keep accurate" notes, the ATS résumé rules and the résumé text layout. Your current pay is never included. It tells the assistant to leave X/Twitter fields blank, never to claim a degree, and to ask you about salary and anything missing (phone, mailing address, referral or non-compete questions).
- `save_tailored_resume`: checks the tailored résumé and refuses it if it lists a degree or an education section while your Profile keeps education off the résumé, names an X account, lacks an EXPERIENCE heading or an email, or uses characters a parser cannot read. It warns about a county or metro area instead of a city on the contact line (Lever's parser maps regions to the nearest city and can misplace you), role dates without a month, acronyms never spelled out (some parsers index only the written form), figures that are not in your Profile, employers it does not recognise, non-standard headings, table-like spacing, long or first-person bullets, length, and a few lines spilling onto page two. It reports which of the posting's required skills the résumé covers, which your Profile supports but the résumé leaves out, and which you do not have. A saved version appears under the job's **Versions for this job** and is exported as a text-based PDF, a Word file and plain text to `applications\<job id>-<company>\` next to the database, for example `applications\62-Acme\Alex-Rivera-Resume.pdf`. Each version on the job page also has **PDF for upload** and **Word file** links. The PDF uses standard fonts in one column, so applicant tracking systems read every word in order.
- `track_application`: status Preparing when work starts; Applied once you have seen the employer's confirmation. Applied sets today's date and a follow-up a week later, and logs the submission (with the résumé version) in the job's Activity. It never moves an application backwards.

The résumé rules follow Jobscan's "The Ultimate ATS Guide" (consistent Month YYYY dates, acronyms spelled out once, standard headings, contact details in the body, at least 0.75-inch margins, text-based PDF) and Lever's "Understanding Resume Parsing" help article. Your mailing address and current pay never go into the résumé text.

Any tool also runs from the command line without an MCP client: `job-review-mcp.exe -db "<db>" -call get_application_kit -args "{\"job_id\":62}"`, or `-args-file args.json` for long text such as a résumé; `-call list` lists the tools. The `apply-to-job` Claude Code skill (`..\.claude\skills\apply-to-job\SKILL.md`; `..\AGENTS.md` lists the skills) uses these tools to run a whole application. Your own standing answers go in `owner.md` beside it, copied from `owner.example.md`; it is git-ignored.

`save_tailored_resume` takes an optional `variant` and `track_application` an optional `resume_variant`: the label of the résumé version, used by the Metrics page A/B comparison. Reuse the exact label for every application that uses the same version.

The other tools: `search_jobs`, `get_job`, `create_job`, `update_job`, `list_applications`, `update_application`, `save_interview`, `save_communication`, `save_document` (cover letters), `set_base_resume` and `compare_resume`. Each job keeps two dates: `posting_date`, when the employer published the listing (pass it to `create_job` or `update_job` as YYYY-MM-DD when the posting shows it), and `date_found`, the day it was added to the tracker, which `create_job` sets. Next up shows both with their age, and the Jobs list can sort by newest listing or recently added. None of them sends email or submits applications. Connected assistants can read private tracker data and edit the database, so connect only clients you trust.

**Claude Desktop and Claude Code** run it over standard input/output. Give an absolute database path, because clients start it from another folder. For Claude Desktop, add this under `"mcpServers"` in `%APPDATA%\Claude\claude_desktop_config.json` and restart Claude Desktop:

```json
"job-search": {
  "command": "C:\\path\\to\\job-search\\review-app\\job-review-mcp.exe",
  "args": ["-mcp", "-db", "C:\\path\\to\\job-search\\job_search.sqlite3"]
}
```

For Claude Code, run `claude mcp add --scope user job-search -- "C:\path\to\job-search\review-app\job-review-mcp.exe" -mcp -db "C:\path\to\job-search\job_search.sqlite3"`. Then ask, for example, "Use job-search to tailor my résumé for job 62 and help me apply."

**ChatGPT** connects only to servers on the internet over HTTPS. Run `job-review-mcp.exe -mcp-http 127.0.0.1:8765 -db "<absolute path>"`. It logs a local address ending in a secret `/mcp/<token>` path, made once and kept in the database. Expose the port with a tunnel such as `cloudflared tunnel --url http://127.0.0.1:8765`. Then turn on developer mode for connectors in ChatGPT and add a connector whose URL is the tunnel's `https://` address plus the same `/mcp/<token>` path, with no authentication. Every other path answers 404. Anyone who learns the full URL can read and change your job search data, including the current pay in your Profile, so stop the tunnel when you are done. To change the secret, delete the `mcp_http_token` row from `tracker_settings`.
